## Why

Polaris must explain the whole Butlers project, not one capability.

- The owner-led cold-open walkthrough found that Polaris did not explain
  Butlers. It presented one WhatsApp capability as the whole surface, and its
  meta-language made a thin model harder to read.
- This change therefore makes project-wide comprehension and complete
  declared-shape coverage explicit POC evaluation criteria.

## What Changes

Polaris opens with the project, accounts for every declared item, keeps every
gap visible, and reads Butlers only under owner authority.

- **The project comes first.** Polaris opens with a concise project account:
  purpose, promises, non-goals, architecture, V1 scope and success criteria.
  - Capability material is drill-down content beneath that account. The
    existing WhatsApp slice is a proving example, not the definition of
    Butlers.
  - An opening aggregate over the evaluation's Unknown project-shape claims
    may appear before the first capability catalog; if it does, there is
    exactly one, and it displaces no project-level category.
- **Every declared item is counted.** The shared model accounts for every
  declared staffer, domain butler, module, connector and major interface in
  the configured Butlers revision.
  - Coverage reports a complete denominator. Missing or unreadable
    declarations stay visible as Unknown and never leave the known-source
    count.
  - A project's loaded profile declares how its items are read, from closed
    lists of container shapes and item key forms. Butlers keeps a built-in
    grammar only until its profile is declared, and a loaded profile has no
    built-in fallback.
- **Conflicts stay visible.** Project-fact identity and precedence use a
  closed grammar.
  - The live eight-versus-nine domain-butler disagreement is one required
    fact in that population.
  - Both declarations stay visible, and no winner is invented when Butlers
    declares no applicable precedence rule.
- **Reads need owner authority.** No repository body is read until three
  artifacts each carry an effective exact-digest human owner act under
  RFC3-16(a): the Butlers observation-consent record, the observing project's
  concrete secret-detection/classification policy, and the project-shape
  observer's registered adapter entry.
  - Each act may be state (1), `owner-adopted (bootstrap, uncorrelated)`, or
    state (2), `Syzygy-verified`. Mixed valid states are allowed, and each
    exact state stays visible.
  - In state (1), Syzygy trusts the reviewed tree: the record stays same-tree
    forgeable from Syzygy's perspective, and its digest detects drift rather
    than authorship or attendance. Only state (2) is independently verified.
  - Missing or invalid acts fail closed.
- **Reads are bounded and inert.** Shape reads take exact Git objects only,
  with no symlink or submodule escape, no active browser content and no
  execution.
  - Inline code spans and fenced code blocks are inert Markdown contexts:
    markup-like examples inside them do not make a source active content.
  - Secret screening still examines their bytes before parsing or rendering.
- **Every claim carries its epistemic state.** Every project claim carries
  its complete epistemic tuple and a minimal, typed source-anchor set, and
  Polaris stays explicitly non-citable.
  - A claim class with no effective currency-bound declaration carries no
    fabricated freshness value. The missing bound is disclosed outside that
    slot, and the claim stays Unknown with its exact reason and route.
  - A claim that renders Unknown may carry a recorded, attributed human
    dismissal with a reason and an expiry, committed to the governed plane.
    Each evaluation decides the dismissal at its own as-of instant, so it
    lapses only through a new evaluation. It replaces the claim's status
    rendering, never its facts, and never counts as resolved or favourable.
- **Every catalog item opens in depth.** Each declared catalog item has one
  detail with argument, exact-contract and observed-reality bands.
  - The contract band carries a separate governing-intent relation claim
    with its own tuple. It stays Unknown until a relation source is admitted
    and a currency bound applies to its class.
  - Proposals appear only in a matching capability's detail, visibly
    separate and unable to grant status.
- **Exact sources stay reachable.** The exact-source route serves each
  admitted source in one of two render modes, `requirement-sections` for a
  baseline spec and `whole-body` for every other admitted source, behind the
  same gates. A withheld source is never served.
- **Proposed work stays subordinate.** Active and proposed OpenSpec work
  appears only within the affected capability's drill-down. It does not
  dominate the project-level narrative.
- **Copy is short and direct.** Polaris uses short headings and direct project
  language. It does not narrate the page's own structure or use prose to
  restate evidence mechanics.
- **Human and machine views match.** The machine answer carries every fact
  Polaris presents. Two closed categories of derived, read-only machine view
  are served beside it, and neither replaces it.
- **Accessibility is part of comprehension.** Every distinction and source
  path stays text-recoverable and keyboard-operable, including a nonvisual
  cold-open run.
- **Success is judged by the owner.** The POC evaluation includes an owner
  cold-open walkthrough that tests whether the reader can explain Butlers as
  a whole.
  - Cold-open readiness retains the complete nine-answer population and
    checks its Polaris-only paths under PWB-REQ-021 before an owner judgment
    is offered. This adds no case to PWB-REQ-022's separate act-validity
    denominator.
  - The criterion accepts an exact-scope owner judgment in either valid
    provenance state, renders the exact state, and treats the verdict as
    human judgment rather than Observed evidence.

The change is additive. It does not edit or weaken POC-REQ-030…032: the
existing narrative, provenance and Unknown obligations continue to apply to
the larger model.

## Impact

The change reaches the shared model, Polaris, the machine answer and the
tests.

- `packages/three-surface-poc-core`: the model gains project-shape source,
  coverage and catalog facts derived from the configured Butlers revision.
- `apps/three-surface-poc`: Polaris becomes the project-level entry and keeps
  the existing capability material as drill-down.
- `GET /api/poc`: the same project-wide facts and coverage denominator become
  machine-readable; human/machine parity stays mandatory.
- Tests gain exhaustive source, coverage, parity, plain-copy and cold-open
  evaluation oracles.

## Scope

The change covers one configured Butlers repository and the project shape its
authoritative artifacts declare.

- **In scope.** “All of Butlers” means all project shape declared by its
  authoritative project artifacts: purpose and boundaries, architecture, V1
  scope and success criteria, and the declared staffer, domain-butler,
  module, connector and major-interface catalogs.
- **Out of scope:** reading arbitrary implementation-file contents; claiming
  that every source file or active proposal is a project capability;
  inferring missing capabilities; modifying Butlers; supporting another
  project; production release; autonomous behavior; multi-user support; or
  weakening visible Unknown, provenance, parity, authentication or
  one-repository POC boundaries.

## Authority and sign-off

Signing this specification, or any amendment to it, authorizes no
implementation by itself.

- The classification policy, the observer registry entry and the consent
  record are separately act-bound authorities. Changing any of their bytes
  needs its own later act.
- Implementation runs only under a separate owner authorization. The
  specification widens neither the consented repository nor the content
  class, and grants no write, egress, execution, deployment, release,
  recovery or mission authority.
- The six signed `three-surface-poc-experience` artifacts are unchanged by
  this change.
