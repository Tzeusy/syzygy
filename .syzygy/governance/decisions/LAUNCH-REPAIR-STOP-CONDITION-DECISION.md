## The ruling — arm (a), N=2

**The repair↔administration cycle is bounded: at most two further
administrations.** Counting from the Administration 1 record
(2026-08-18):

- Administration 2 and, if needed, Administration 3 may be
  commissioned.
- **If Administration 3 is not `READY`, the owner makes the launch
  decision directly on the record then in hand.** The cycle ends either
  way — by a pass verdict, or by the owner's direct decision at the
  bound.

This is a stop condition **other than exhaustion**, in the instrument's
F1 sense: what would end the cycle is now stateable in one sentence.

**What the bound does and does not constrain:** it constrains *when*
the launch decision stops being deferred to another administration —
never *what* that decision is. At the bound, "no launch" and scope
changes remain fully available decisions; the owner's freedom over the
decision's content is untouched.

## Standing

- The stop condition **schedules nothing and commissions nothing**.
  Administration 2 remains a separate commissioning choice.
- It answers the stop-condition half of F1's settlement; the other half
  (a second full formal administration at a descendant commit,
  declaring the 2026-08-18 record as its prior) remains to be performed.
- Relation to F6: this bounds one loop in one unit (administrations).
  It is evidence toward F6's concern, not the general governance-effort
  ceiling F6 asks for — which the owner declined to declare, knowingly
  ([`F6-GOVERNANCE-CEILING-DECISION.md`](F6-GOVERNANCE-CEILING-DECISION.md)).

## Boundary

This record sets no administration calendar, pre-commits nothing about
the launch decision's content at the bound, amends neither the
instrument nor F1's settlement text, and authorizes no OpenSpec
authoring, implementation, runtime, external, privileged, or destructive
action. The condition applies at real future commissioning choices only.
Where this record and the owning packet disagree, the packet's boundary
wins.
