# Knowledge hygiene — candidate craft policy (`CC-KNOW-*`, `CC-BUDGET-*`)

Syzygy's craft cluster has a consistent spine: *a claim needs a retained
artifact; absence renders Unknown, never zero; no actor grants itself an
exemption.* The cluster applies that spine to product surfaces, tests,
provenance, dependencies and security. It has never applied it to **the
project's own governance documents** — which is the gap this policy fills.
That is a narrower claim than "here are eighteen new rules," and it is the
right one: most clauses below are the governance-lane application of an
obligation the cluster already carries elsewhere, and say so.

## Scope and non-scope

**Scope.** How the project's own knowledge is kept true: where a fact lives,
how vocabulary enters, how normative text changes, what relation derived
views bear to their sources, what enters a reading path, and when a
document's size triggers decomposition review. It reaches the **active
governance lane** (doctrine, contract modules, policy, topology, owner
decisions, front-door files) and every derived view over it.

**Non-scope.** What any rule should *say* — doctrine and the contracts own
that. The owner acceptance ceremony for gate-bound artifacts — the active
acceptance record owns it. The content of the term registry — a sibling
candidate, cited here as a mechanism, never as authority. Enforcement
tooling — none of these rules is script-checked today. Code and runtime
documentation once implementation exists — the rest of the cluster reaches
there.

## Relationship to the installed cluster

Where an installed clause already owns an obligation, this policy **cites it
and does not restate it**; a restated rule drifts and becomes the shadow
authority CC-REV-3 forbids. Rules that narrow or extend an installed clause
carry an explicit *Extends:* line, and add only the governance-lane
obligation that the installed clause does not state. Precedence is the
cluster README's, unchanged: adopted doctrine and owner rulings > this
cluster > the canonical bar.

## 1. Homes, scope, and warrants

### CC-KNOW-1 — A governance fact is stated at its one home, or cited

Every fact in an active governance artifact is either stated at the single
home the typed-authority table assigns it, or is a **citation naming that
home** — including in routers, indexes, status pages and front-door files,
which are the surfaces most tempted to paraphrase for convenience.
*Extends:* **CC-REV-3** (single authoritative home) to the project's own
governance corpus.

*Violation:* an agent router paraphrasing a doctrine rule "so agents don't
have to open the file," after which agents obey the paraphrase and nothing
re-points them when the doctrine text is amended.

### CC-KNOW-2 — Every active artifact states its scope and its non-scope

Non-scope is not optional: an artifact that states only what it covers
invites readers to assume it covers the adjacent thing.

*Violation:* a contract module listing what it governs and staying silent on
the neighbouring concern, so two modules each assume the other owns it and
neither does.

### CC-KNOW-3 — Active artifacts carry current obligations; history is cited, not carried

An active artifact states what is required **now**; superseded wording, prior
revisions, negotiation narrative and rationale history live in the historical
lane and are reachable by citation. History inline costs every reader the
work of deciding which sentence still binds.

*Violation:* a contract module retaining "in the prior revision this clause
read …" inline, so a fresh reader must reconstruct an amendment history
before learning today's obligation.

### CC-KNOW-4 — No active artifact depends solely on founder-local history

Every active-lane artifact — **and every warrant it names as the basis of its
own authority** — resolves in a fresh public clone; a basis that resolves
only on the founder machine is a recorded defect, not a convenience.
*Extends:* **CC-PROV-3** (nothing preserved may require an expiring
transcript to resolve) from evidence retention to governance warrants.

*Violation:* an approval record whose sole stated warrant is a git-excluded
path, so a clone reader can verify that the change happened but not that it
was authorized. [Observed — the FD-037 warrant extractions and citation
repoints exist because active records were in this state.]

## 2. Vocabulary

### CC-KNOW-5 — Terms are scarce

A new durable term is admitted only when no existing term covers it, its
distinction matters operationally, its owning authority and lifecycle are
clear, and a technically capable newcomer can explain it after one paragraph.
Vocabulary growth is not free: every term added is a term every future reader
must hold.

*Violation:* introducing "artifact health" alongside the already-defined
evidence, freshness and status vocabulary — two words for one concept, and
the new one has no owner.

### CC-KNOW-6 — New vocabulary enters through the term registry; replacement needs a migration report

The candidate **term registry** — offered as a sibling candidate in this
directory, and itself a derived record rather than an authority — is the
admission mechanism: a term used
normatively in an active artifact without a registry entry is an unadmitted
term, and a term that carries obligation while no clause defines it is a
**contract gap to report** — never a gap for a registry, index or summary to
fill, because a derived artifact supplying a definition manufactures
authority. Renaming or retiring a term across artifacts requires a reviewed
migration report *before* the sweep. (CC-REV-7 governs stable identifiers;
this clause governs words.)

*Violation:* a registry inventing a plausible definition for a term no clause
defines, which becomes operative by repetition.

## 3. Normative change

### CC-KNOW-7 — Normative edits travel as semantic deltas

A change to active-lane normative text is proposed as a semantic delta
recording current meaning, proposed meaning, change class, warrant, evidence
basis, terms moved, downstream impact, what explicitly does not change, the
migration plan, and the review class. Protocol: the sibling
`SEMANTIC-DELTA-TEMPLATE.md` and `NORMATIVE-CHANGE-WORKFLOW.md`.

*Violation:* a "small wording fix" to a contract clause landing as a bare
diff, so no later reader can tell whether the obligation moved.

### CC-KNOW-8 — "Editorial" and "no semantic change" are reviewable claims

They are claims to be tested, never exemptions from review; the class is
stated up front precisely so a reviewer can contradict it. Class is
determined by what changes in the obligation, never by diff size.

*Violation:* a one-line edit labeled Editorial that swaps the precondition on
which a policy binds — one line, and a different obligation. [Observed — a
retired acceptance phrase named as a live binding condition, repaired this
round.]

### CC-KNOW-9 — Material normative change gets fresh-context review

A material change to an active-lane artifact — **including candidate
governance material and this policy** — is reviewed by a session that neither
authored it nor holds the authoring context, and that receives the artifact,
its governing references and the acceptance criteria, not the author's
reasoning or a desired verdict. *Extends:* **CC-REV-1** and **CC-REV-4** to
the governance corpus itself, which is where self-review is likeliest to go
unnoticed.

*Violation:* an agent drafting a policy, re-reading it later in the same
session, and recording "reviewed" — self-review wearing a review's label.
[Observed — the pending-decision register records this pass's own drafts as
unreviewed by any fresh session.]

### CC-KNOW-10 — Whole-file normative rewrites are exceptional and carry a mapping

Replacing an active normative file wholesale requires stated justification
and an old→new mapping covering every stable identifier; structural moves
preserve identifiers. A full rewrite conceals which meanings changed — that
is its defect, independent of intent. *Extends:* **CC-BAR-7** (changes stay
reviewable) with the documentation-specific mapping requirement.

*Violation:* a "clarity rewrite" of a contract module that drops one clause;
the diff is unreadable end to end, so the drop surfaces only when someone
cites the missing identifier.

## 4. Derived views and current state

### CC-KNOW-11 — Generated and summary artifacts are never authority

Indexes, maps, manifests, routers, status pages and summaries are **derived**:
each is labeled derived, names its source, and regenerates **after** the
source is adopted — never in anticipation of an approval that has not
happened.

*Violation:* a generated context-load map carrying two stale cells beneath a
sentence claiming every figure is script output. [Observed — portability
review finding 2, this pass.]

### CC-KNOW-12 — Current-state prose is generated or revision-scoped

Prose asserting what is currently true carries its as-of revision and is not
the sole source for the fact it states; prefer a generated or queryable view,
because a current-state section that must be hand-edited whenever reality
changes is a known decay site. (CC-REV-5 governs epistemic labelling; this
governs currency.)

*Violation:* a status file describing "the current gates" in three
hand-maintained places, two of them a revision behind, none of them saying as
of when it was true.

## 5. What enters a reading path

### CC-KNOW-13 — Context is compiled, not accumulated

A task loads the minimum artifact set needed for one correct decision, named
in advance as a packet, rather than a growing pile of everything read so far;
"read the governance tree first" is not a context strategy.

*Violation:* an agent told to load the whole governance directory, which
spends its budget on material irrelevant to its decision and still misses the
one clause that governs it.

### CC-KNOW-14 — Superseded material leaves the default reading path in the same change

When material is superseded, retired or moved to the historical lane, the
**same logical change** removes it from every default reading path, index and
router. Retention is not the problem; reachability by a reader who does not
know it was superseded is. *Extends:* **CC-REV-2** (same-logical-change) to
reading paths and derived views.

*Violation:* a retired acceptance phrase still named as the binding
precondition by two tracked governance files a revision after its retirement
— the retirement was correct and the propagation never happened. [Observed —
repaired this round.]

## 6. Reproducibility and derivation

### CC-KNOW-15 — Clone reproducibility is part of done for a governance change

A governance change is done only when a fresh public clone can read the
changed artifacts, resolve their citations, and run the repository's checks
without founder-local material — verified in that clone, not asserted from
the working tree.

*Violation:* a governance change verified where the git-excluded bootstrap
tree exists; the tracked result cites five paths a clone does not have, and
nothing in the change record says the check was run anywhere else.

### CC-KNOW-16 — Completeness claims require the sweep, in the same session

"All," "none," "zero," "100%" may be written only if the exact sweep
described was run against current content in the same session; otherwise
enumerate what is known and state the remainder as unknown. A sweep that
returns nothing supports a universal claim only when a second method agrees —
a silently-failing pattern and a genuinely empty corpus are indistinguishable
from the output.

*Violation:* "all links resolve," written from the memory of a sweep run two
revisions and one fix batch ago.

### CC-KNOW-17 — Figures carry their derivation; totals and digests are computed

Any quantitative claim in an active or governed-presentation artifact states
how it was computed; **group totals are computed by script, never
hand-summed, and digests are scripted, never hand-transcribed.** After a
batch of edits, the self-referential counts of *every file the batch touched*
are re-verified — not only those whose fix was itself a count.

*Violation:* a file stating its own word count, correct when written, stale
the moment a later section was added — with three downstream figures derived
from it. [Observed — a residual of this kind rides in the current candidate
package; and two hand-transcribed digest incidents preceded it.]

### CC-KNOW-18 — A new check's first result is a claim about the checker

The first result of a validation check against a corpus it has not run
against before is a **hypothesis about the checker** until at least one hit
is confirmed by reading the source — and a passing check proves nothing until
it has been shown able to fail on that corpus. Every check reports its
**denominator** and the **class it covers**: a truthful count over the wrong
population is still the wrong answer, and a zero-count check reports WARN,
never PASS.

*Violation:* a citation checker reporting dozens of unresolved citations in a
frozen corpus, every one an artifact of unreachable definition sources; and
its mirror image, a link check reporting "all resolve" while blind to the
syntax carrying the defect. [Observed — three such incidents in this pass's
own check battery, none of which survived reading the source.]

## 7. Context budgets

### CC-BUDGET-1 — Budget triggers

| Artifact | Budget trigger |
|---|---:|
| Root README | 1,200 words |
| AGENTS | 1,500 words |
| Active RFC module | 4,000 words |
| Exceptional active module | focused decomposition review above 5,000 words |
| Default context packet | 5,000–15,000 tokens |
| Context packet above 20,000 tokens | justification or task decomposition |

### CC-BUDGET-2 — Budgets are decomposition triggers, not validity laws

Crossing a budget triggers a **decomposition review**, not a failure: an
artifact over budget is not thereby invalid, and an artifact under budget is
not thereby well-formed. The review asks whether an honest split exists;
"none does" is a lawful outcome, recorded rather than hidden.

*Violation:* a module split into two files just under the trigger, sharing a
preamble and always read together — the number is satisfied and the reader's
load is unchanged.

### CC-BUDGET-3 — Waivers are recorded, with an expiry

A waiver records artifact, reason, scope, reviewer, and an expiry or revisit
trigger; the verifier prints the recorded justification alongside the breach
rather than passing silently. A waiver with no expiry is a permanent
exception pretending to be temporary.

*Violation:* "we accept that this module is long" appended to a check's
allowlist, with no reason, no reviewer and no revisit — after which the
breach is invisible to everyone who did not read the allowlist.

### CC-BUDGET-4 — Budgets bound the active lane only

Historical, evidence and scratch lanes carry no budget; what is budgeted is
what enters a default reading path. Retained history may grow without limit.

*Violation:* deleting review evidence to bring a directory "under budget" —
trading a real record for a number that never applied to it.

## Change log — this candidate against the prior draft

The prior draft — never adopted, and named here only as this candidate's
derivation —
`_bootstrap/knowledge-refactor/CRAFT-KNOWLEDGE-HYGIENE-POLICY-DRAFT.md`, historical,
git-excluded, unavailable to a clone — differs as follows.
Nothing in this policy requires reading it.

1. **Budget baselines reconciled to the charter's table.** The draft's budget
   table carried nine rows with lower bounds and extra artifact classes (one
   doctrine file 1,000–2,500 words; total active doctrine ~8,000–12,000; one
   capability spec 8–15 requirements; active contract module 1,500–4,000;
   root README 500–1,200). CC-BUDGET-1 above carries the charter's six-row
   table as written, and drops the rest. Reason: the dropped rows had no
   owner basis, and at least one adopted doctrine file and the active
   doctrine total already sit outside them — a candidate policy that puts
   adopted material in permanent breach is a defect in the policy. The
   surviving upper triggers are unchanged in substance (4,000 for an active
   module; decomposition review above 5,000; the packet figures verbatim);
   only the lower bounds and the extra classes are gone. The draft's own
   caveat that these figures are unvalidated against this repository still
   stands, and CC-BUDGET-2 is why that is tolerable.
2. **All sixteen charter items are covered.** The draft dropped six of them
   as already covered by installed clauses. This candidate covers all
   sixteen, but each such rule is written as the **governance-lane
   application** of the installed clause, carries an explicit *Extends:*
   citation, and restates none of it — so the charter's coverage requirement
   and CC-REV-3 are both satisfied. The rules in that position are CC-KNOW-1,
   CC-KNOW-4, CC-KNOW-9, CC-KNOW-10 and CC-KNOW-14.
3. **Identifiers renumbered.** These IDs do not correspond to the draft's
   provisional `CC-KNOW-1…12`. Permitted because the draft was never adopted
   and CC-REV-7 attaches at adoption; **any surviving citation of
   "CC-KNOW-n" that predates this file refers to the draft's numbering, not
   to these rules.** The pending-decision register's review item names the
   draft numbering in exactly this way.
4. **No `KA-*` identifiers.** The draft-era workflow file cited a `KA-12`
   from a proposed contract that does not exist in the candidate corpus;
   every such reference is repointed to the clause here that owns the
   obligation (CC-KNOW-11). Lifecycle is described with words — adopted,
   approved, candidate, derived, historical, retired — never with a class
   number.
5. **Three clauses kept beyond the charter's sixteen.** CC-KNOW-16,
   CC-KNOW-17 and CC-KNOW-18 are the draft's incident-derived clauses. They
   are knowledge hygiene and have no other home; they are listed separately
   above so they can be struck in one act without disturbing the sixteen.
6. **Drafting narrative removed.** The draft's audit rounds, self-assessment
   and patch log are process evidence, not policy; they stay in the
   historical draft and are not carried into an artifact meant to bind.

This file states **no self-referential word count**: a count written inline
is stale the moment the file is edited, which is CC-KNOW-17's own violation
example. The count is computed by script when the policy is offered. Note for
that computation: everything from the coverage table down — that table, this
change log, the stated limits, the ruling items — is **proposal apparatus**,
written for the reviewing session and the owner act rather than for a
standing reader. Whether it travels into the craft cluster on installation or
stays with the candidate record is ruling item 5.

## What this policy does not do

- **It states no enforcement mechanism.** CC-KNOW-16, CC-KNOW-17,
  CC-BUDGET-1 and parts of CC-KNOW-4 are script-checkable and are not
  scripted here.
- **Its overlap with the installed cluster has not been audited by a fresh
  session.** The draft's audits were self-audits by the clauses' author; the
  first round of that self-audit found six duplicates, which is the reason
  the check cannot be the author's. CC-KNOW-16, CC-KNOW-17 and CC-KNOW-18
  in particular sit near CC-BAR-2(4), CC-TEST-3 and CC-TEST-4 and have never
  been checked against them in full text.
- **Overlap with adopted doctrine and with the canonical bar adopted by
  reference is unchecked.**
- **CC-KNOW-5's admission test has not been applied to existing
  vocabulary.** Every current term is incumbent, admitted by prior use.
- **It has never been applied.** No governance change has yet been made under
  these rules end to end.
