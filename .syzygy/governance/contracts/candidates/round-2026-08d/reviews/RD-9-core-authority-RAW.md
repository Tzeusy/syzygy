## Findings

**1. RFC1-9's minting discipline does not reach the entities RFC1-7 exists to admit, and RFC1-7 requires no plane assignment for them — so RFC6-19 class 1 is unsatisfiable for a profile-minted entity. Severity: major.**

Anchor — RFC1-9: *"Every identity-bearing class names, **in RFC1-5**, exactly one minting authority, and the kernel **never mints an identity it does not own** … Kernel-owned identities (snapshot, evaluation, claim, gap, contradiction, materialization record) are **deterministically derived** from their defining inputs."* Anchor — RFC1-7: *"Extension profiles are named, per-project-loadable entity and relation sets, never presumed present … A profile may add vocabulary; it may never alter the semantics of a V0-core clause … a profile-defining RFC names the minting authority of every identity it adds, under RFC1-9's discipline."* Anchor — RFC1-22: *"Every **source-state assertion** in the graph … is assigned to exactly one of six **state planes**"*. Anchor — RFC6-19 class 1: *"…and the **state plane** each rendered fact belongs to (desired, proposed, observed, inferred, execution, historical — RFC1-22's assignment)."*

Two distinct gaps. (a) RFC1-9 requires the minting authority to be named *in RFC1-5*, and RFC1-5 is the closed V0-core table which by RFC1-7's own terms can never list a profile entity; RFC1-9's enumeration of kernel-owned classes is likewise closed and excludes them. RFC1-7 delegates the naming, but nothing licenses a *new* kernel-owned class, which is exactly what RFC10-4's *"the minting authority is the control-plane service at approval submission, deterministic over the objective, target, and initiating act"* creates. [Observed] The seam shows in RFC-0010's own two statements: RFC10-4 says *"minted under RFC 0001's identity rules … the minting authority is the control-plane service"* while RFC10-12 says only *"minted by the control-plane service"* — one file reads the delegation as sufficient, the other does not invoke RFC1-9 at all. (b) RFC1-7 requires a profile RFC to name minting authority and nothing else. It does not require it to name the **state plane** of each assertion it adds, yet RFC1-22 demands exactly one plane for every source-state assertion and RFC6-19 class 1 demands the drawer render it. Sweep with denominator: RFC1-22's plane table has six rows; none of their Contents cells admits a profile-added entity, and RFC1-23's act-assignment rule governs *acts*, not entity classes — so no clause in RFC 0001 determines a Mission's or an Attention Item's plane.

Repair that would close it: amend RFC1-7 so its delegation sentence reads on both dimensions — a profile-defining RFC must name, for every identity it adds, (i) exactly one minting authority, which if kernel-owned must be deterministically derived from declared inputs in RFC1-9's sense, and (ii) exactly one RFC1-22 state plane; and amend RFC1-9 to say that its "in RFC1-5" locator is discharged by RFC1-7 for profile classes, so a reader of RFC1-9 alone does not conclude that every profile entity violates it.

**2. RFC6-19 class 7 makes "the coverage boundary" a mandatory drawer fact and anchors it to a clause that does not define it. Severity: major.**

Anchor — RFC6-19 class 7: *"**Policy visibility** — exclusions (with counts), the **coverage boundary** (what the producing evaluation could and could not observe, RFC4-2), and consent state (§3.8)."* Anchor — the cited clause, RFC4-2 in full scope: *"**Mandatory declaration set.** Every observer/adapter declares, in its registry entry (RFC4-7), before any output is admissible:"* followed by exactly seven items — identity, versions, declared inputs, declared outputs, determinism class, failure states, authority boundary. [Observed] Sweep: the string `coverage` appears **0 times** in `RFC-0004/general-contract.md`, the file that owns RFC4-1…RFC4-9. RFC4-2 is a registry-declaration clause about an adapter's static declarations; it says nothing about what a *producing evaluation* could and could not observe. The material that does exist is elsewhere and is narrower: RFC4-27 (*"An absence claim … requires an executed **mapping coverage record**: which mapping pass ran, over which declared scope (repositories, exclusions counted), with which observer/adapter versions, at which evaluation, and what it found"*) and RFC2-6 (*"coverage records for any executed mapping or oracle"*). This is a normative obligation resting on section-adjacent reading rather than a defined clause.

Repair that would close it: re-anchor class 7 to RFC4-27 and RFC2-6, and either define "coverage boundary" as the union of the evaluation's coverage records and its RFC2-23 partial-snapshot declared scope, or replace the term with those two defined constructs.

**3. RFC1-1 discharges the zero-governance-root contradiction into a "workspace's own evaluation context" that no clause in the corpus defines, and that RFC3-10/RFC3-12 arguably forbid. Severity: major.**

Anchor — RFC1-1: *"**Zero roots** is not [evaluable]: with no designated governance root there is no declared home to read the declaration from and no snapshot anchor for it, hence no Project evaluation in which to mint anything. That case surfaces at the **workspace/manifest level** — unevaluable as a Project, rendered Unknown (`missing-declaration`) in the observing workspace, with the contradiction minted in the **workspace's own evaluation context** (RFC 0003's manifest rendering — informative, §5)"*. Anchor — RFC2-3: *"A status evaluation is identified by the pair **(source snapshot, as-of instant)** — and nothing else."* Anchor — RFC3-10: *"It is classified under **VIS-6, exception (a)** — personal presentation state: it may never affect truth, work, status, or certificates, and it is never a snapshot input (RFC2-1)."* Anchor — RFC3-12: *"No kernel fact, claim, gap, consent, capability, or relation may cite the workspace manifest as its source."* Anchor — what RFC 0003 actually does in this case, RFC3-13: *"An entry whose project identifier cannot be resolved (repository unreachable, plane absent, consent withdrawn) renders Unknown with its RFC2-24 reason — never dropped, never guessed."*

[Observed] Sweep with denominator: across all 39 files under `C/rfcs/**`, the only line matching `workspace` alongside `evaluat|snapshot|contradiction` is RFC1-1's own. No clause defines a workspace-level evaluation, a workspace-level snapshot, or a workspace-level contradiction-minting context. A Contradiction requires an evaluation identity (RFC1-18(b): *"**Evaluation instance** — durable identity + evaluation identity"*), evaluations are project-snapshot-identified (RFC2-3), and the workspace manifest is barred from being a snapshot input (RFC3-10) or the source of a kernel fact (RFC3-12). RFC1-1 marks the forward reference informative, which protects RFC 0001's meaning but leaves the obligation undischarged: the cited RFC 0003 rendering renders Unknown and mints nothing. The safe half of the behaviour (Unknown, `missing-declaration`) is agreed by both contracts, so this is not a greenness risk — it is an unrealizable clause.

Repair that would close it: either drop the contradiction-minting half of RFC1-1's zero-roots sentence and state that the case renders Unknown (`missing-declaration`) at the workspace with no kernel contradiction minted — which is what RFC3-13 already does — or add to RFC 0003 a defined workspace-level evaluation construct with its own snapshot, and reconcile it explicitly with RFC3-10's "never a snapshot input".

**4. The `supersedes` typed-relation set cannot be enumerated from the RFC1-25 table, contradicting RFC1-25(d)'s claim that the table *is* the enumeration. Severity: major.**

Anchor — RFC1-25 `supersedes` row: *"Same-class pairs only: for each identity-bearing class whose RFC1-31 lifecycle mints successive versions or superseding records, exactly the pair (that class → prior version of the same class); no cross-class pair exists"*. Anchor — RFC1-25(d): *"each row contributes one typed relation **per endpoint pair it lists** … The table — read this way — **is** the enumeration of V0 typed relations; no separate list exists to drift from it … the typed-relation set is closed *because* the rows and their endpoint pairs are closed"*, and *"Each typed relation's identity is a **stable typed relation identifier** … every typed relation carries as explicit metadata its **ordered endpoint domains** … and its **semantic relation class**"*.

The `supersedes` row lists no endpoint pair. Its pairs are delegated to RFC1-31's prose, which has six lifecycle groups; sweeping them: **determinate** — Governance acts (*"recorded/granted → in force → expired/narrowed/revoked/superseded"*) yields Decision, Consent record and Policy pairs; Observed classes yields Observation record (*"observation records are immutable and become historical when superseded"*); Proposals yields Proposal (*"rejected / abandoned / superseded"*). **Indeterminate** — Declared classes are *"amended in place (same identifier)"* with no version minting stated, so whether Capability→Capability is a typed relation is unanswerable; Work items have their *"lifecycle owned by the scheduler"*, and RFC2-20 names `superseded` among scheduler closure states, so Work item→Work item is likewise unanswerable. Two of six groups indeterminate. An implementation therefore cannot mint the stable identifiers and endpoint-domain metadata RFC1-25(d) requires without making a governance decision, and RFC1-26's closure claim (*"the typed-relation set is closed because the rows and their endpoint pairs are closed"*) is not true of this row.

Repair that would close it: enumerate the `supersedes` endpoint pairs explicitly in the row, as every other row does, and state for each of RFC1-31's six lifecycle groups whether it versions.

**5. RFC6-22's equivalence tuple omits the two fields RFC6-19 class 8 makes mandatory, so a work-state or chain-state disagreement between two renderings is not release-blocking. Severity: major.**

Anchor — RFC6-19 class 8: *"**Work and reconciliation state** — where work bears on the selection, the relevant items' normalized work state (RFC8-12) and chain state (RFC8-28), carried as two fields, never folded, and never rendered as proof of satisfaction (work is never proof); and the selection's reconciliation state (RFC2-18, RFC2-19) — uncomputed reconciliation renders Unknown, never green."* Anchor — RFC6-22: *"Two renderings … are **equivalent** iff they present: the same evaluation identity, the same declared filters, the same underlying graph (entities and edges), the same epistemic states (label + tier + reason + freshness), the same sibling surface states …, and the same scenario context (RFC6-24) for every presented element."* Anchor — RFC6-23: *"What no pair of equivalent renderings may do is disagree: on an entity's existence, an edge, a label, a tier, a reason, a freshness state, a sibling surface state, a scenario context, or a count over the same declared scope. Any such disagreement is a kernel or projection defect, release-blocking under the trust floor."*

Neither enumeration contains work state or chain state. Every other RFC6-19 class maps onto RFC6-22's tuple; class 8 does not. [Inferred] The consequence is precise: two conforming surfaces may both carry the two fields (RFC8-28 requires carriage — *"Every rendering, filter, count, endpoint answer, and cross-surface handoff that carries the normalized state carries the chain state beside it"*) and still disagree on their *values* without triggering RFC6-23. That is the "divergent truth by projection" failure RFC-0006's own §2 names, on the one fact class where "work is never proof" bites hardest. A second, smaller ambiguity sits in the same clause: class 8 names "chain state (RFC8-28)" and then "the selection's reconciliation state (RFC2-18, RFC2-19)" after saying "carried as two fields" — [Observed] RFC8-28 defines chain state as exactly the RFC2-18 chain states, so the clause names the same thing twice while committing to a field count.

Repair that would close it: add normalized work state and chain state to RFC6-22's tuple and to RFC6-23's disagreement list; and rewrite class 8 so the field count and the citations agree — two fields (normalized work state, chain state), with RFC2-18/RFC2-19 cited as the definition of the second rather than as a third item.

**6. RFC 0002 asserts three mutually inconsistent things about whether it depends on RFC 0003/0004/0005, and the inconsistency has a security-relevant consequence under some acceptance orders. Severity: major.**

Anchor — front matter, `RFC-0002/README.md`: `depends_on: [RFC-0001, RFC-0003, RFC-0004, RFC-0005]`. Anchor — §5, same file: *"**Relies on RFC 0003:** the RFC3-16 owner-act predicate — RFC3-16(a) gating every authorization-bearing artifact this package names … **Relies on RFC 0004:** RFC4-13 route 4 and its governed-checker requirement (RFC4-13(b)) as a `gate-backed` route for doc-only and governance-only reconciliation. **Relies on RFC 0005:** consent-record representation behind reason #6…"*. Anchor — §5, same file, two paragraphs later: *"**Forward references are informative.** Where this package cites a sibling *draft* by clause number (RFC3-n, RFC4-n, RFC5-n, and the surface RFCs), the citation is **informative until that RFC is accepted**: it names where an obligation will be discharged, never a dependency of this contract's meaning … Only citations to **adopted doctrine**, to the **SDR**, and to **RFC 0001** are load-bearing."*

The same citations are declared a dependency (front matter), a reliance (§5 first paragraph), and *not* a dependency of the contract's meaning (§5 third paragraph). [Inferred] They are reliances in fact: RFC2-18's `reconciled@E` route for doc-only work has no content without RFC4-13(b), and RFC2-9's own text makes its provenance gate conditional — *"honored only under the owner-act provenance predicate (RFC3-16(a), informative until RFC 0003 is accepted)"*. Read literally, an acceptance that binds RFC 0002 before RFC 0003 leaves currency-bound declarations honored without owner-act provenance — precisely the widening RFC2-13 and RFC3-16(a) exist to prevent. The same conditional appears in RFC2-13's authorization-bearing paragraph.

Repair that would close it: name RFC 0003 (at minimum RFC3-16(a)/(b)) load-bearing alongside doctrine, the SDR and RFC 0001 in the forward-reference paragraph, and state that RFC 0002 does not bind unless RFC 0003 is bound at the same act or earlier; or, if the intent is genuinely that the citations are informative, remove RFC-0003/0004/0005 from `depends_on` and remove the conditional "informative until RFC 0003 is accepted" from RFC2-9 and RFC2-13 so the gate is unconditional.

**9. RFC5-5's closed unauthenticated-endpoint set is not interface-scoped, while SEC-1 permits unauthenticated reachability only on loopback. Severity: minor.**

Anchor — SEC-1: *"Syzygy's endpoints and UI are reachable without authentication only on the loopback interface … Any exposure beyond localhost requires authenticated, TLS-protected access limited to the owner's own devices."* Anchor — RFC5-5: *"a request with neither session nor machine credential may reach only the endpoints an unauthenticated visitor may see, and that set is closed: liveness/health and the authentication bootstrap itself, neither exposing portfolio data."* The set carries no interface qualifier, so on an overlay or TLS-LAN exposure mode the health and bootstrap endpoints are reachable unauthenticated — literally outside SEC-1's "only on the loopback interface". [Inferred] Residual risk is small: RFC5-9 requires transport-level device restriction on both non-loopback modes, RFC5-10 keeps portfolio data off them, and RFC5-6 requires credential issuance *"only through an owner-attended ceremony on an already-trusted channel (an authenticated session, or the serving host's own console)"*. The defect is that the contract does not say so, and a conforming implementation could expose a credential-issuance bootstrap on a LAN address on RFC5-5's plain text.

Repair that would close it: qualify RFC5-5's closed set — either scope it to loopback, or state that on non-loopback modes the set is reachable only behind the mode's device restriction and that the bootstrap endpoint never issues a credential outside RFC5-6's ceremony.

**10. Secondary Unknown annotations are permitted by RFC2-24 but have no carriage, drawer, or counting rule in RFC 0006. Severity: minor.**

Anchor — RFC2-24: *"Every Unknown claim instance carries exactly one primary reason from this list (**secondary reasons may annotate**) … A secondary annotation is one or more further reasons **drawn from the twelve below**"*. Anchor — RFC6-5, `unknown` outcome: *"Carry exactly one RFC2-24 reason and its resolution route."* Anchor — RFC6-14: *"the Unknown reason where applicable (RFC2-24)"*. Anchor — RFC6-17: *"per-label, per-tier, per-Unknown-reason and per-freshness-state counts"*. All three speak in the singular. Nothing says whether a machine answer must carry secondaries verbatim, whether the drawer must show them, or whether an instance with a primary and two secondaries contributes one count or three to RFC6-17's per-reason composition. [Inferred] Two conforming surfaces could therefore produce different Unknown-reason counts over one declared scope while both satisfying RFC6-17 — which RFC6-23 would then class as a release-blocking disagreement on "a count over the same declared scope", i.e. the contract makes both behaviours simultaneously required and forbidden.

Repair that would close it: state in RFC6-14 that secondary annotations travel verbatim alongside the primary, and in RFC6-17 that per-Unknown-reason counts are computed over primary reasons only, with secondaries disclosed separately.
