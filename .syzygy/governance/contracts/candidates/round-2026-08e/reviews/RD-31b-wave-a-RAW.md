## 5. New findings

### N-1 — MINOR. RFC3-2's *class characterization* was not widened alongside its *trigger rule*, and trigger 1 is not word-identical to RFC3-15's

The repair widened RFC3-2's third paragraph. Its first two paragraphs — the clause's own definitional text — still describe a one-trigger class. **[Observed]**, quoting the defined clause:

> **kernel-recorded** (values the kernel writes as a factual record of a **non-owner actor's** submission)

> The fourth class, `kernel-recorded` … covers a fact the kernel authors **on someone else's act**: a challenge admission or rejection record, a submitted withdrawal, a walkthrough execution record. … what distinguishes the class is that the **content originates outside the kernel and outside the owner**, so the record asserts *that a submission occurred*, never that its content is true.

A sweep resolution record satisfies none of these three descriptions: it records no submission, its content originates from an owner-approved policy executed by the kernel, and it asserts that a *resolution* occurred. Separately, the new sentence asserts set identity — "the same set RFC3-15's `records/` cell states" — but trigger 1 differs by one qualifier: RFC3-15 says "**non-owner** actor's submission", RFC3-2 says "**an** actor's submission". RFC2-13's admission table admits "an attributed human, via the governance plane" as a challenger, and nothing excludes the owner from that row.

**[Inferred]** Why this is MINOR and not a re-opening of RD31-01: I looked for an operative divergence and found none. On admissibility, the two clauses now agree exactly ("on exactly two triggers, and no others"). On write authority, only `kernel-recorded` is available among RFC3-2's four classes, and RFC3-2's own ¶3 mints the record, so a conforming validator lands there and then finds ¶2's "never authorization-bearing" rule satisfied by ¶3's explicit "the record authorizes nothing of itself". The "non-owner" gap is resolved within RFC3-2 by its next sentence ("**Owner resolution acts** … are Decisions in `decisions/` …, never minted into `records/`"). So this is a characterization lagging its own rule, not two rules in conflict — the difference between this and RD31-01, which was two clauses stating different sets. Note also that RFC3-17(a) names the write authority of admission records explicitly (`kernel-recorded`, RFC3-2's fourth class) while no clause names it for the sweep resolution record. **Recommended for the next pass, not this one** (rule 10): widen ¶1/¶2 to two limbs and align trigger 1's qualifier, the same repair ¶3 already received.
