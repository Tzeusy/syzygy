# Consent-gate act recognition: what each model sees of a record

Evidence report for bead `syzygy-bpyt.8.10` (finding: the runtime consent gate
accepts field-parsed act records whose prose was altered). It measures and
proposes; it changes no gate behaviour and nothing about what counts as a
performed act. It is not authority and binds nothing.

- Measured commit: the `measuredOn.commit` of the companion record
  `docs/evidence/consent-gate-recognition-models-2026-10-21.json`, generated
  by `scripts/measure_consent_gate_recognition.mjs` (which also runs
  `scripts/probe_superseded_policy_act_regeneration.py` and embeds its output).
- Rule-6 records for the characterization tests, one per test project:
  `docs/evidence/consent-gate-recognition-models-mutants-consent-2026-10-21.json`
  and `docs/evidence/consent-gate-recognition-models-mutants-dossier-2026-10-21.json`.
- Authority for the status quo: GP-Q0 Part 2, row "How the consent gate
  recognizes an act", in
  `.syzygy/governance/decisions/OWNER-DIRECTION-GREENFIELD-PARITY-ANSWERS-2026-10-21.md`:
  "**Kept as is.** The two-copy template stays, held by the independent oracle
  test. Bead `8.4` never moves a byte-exact act onto field parsing, and bead
  `2.2` keeps the act-record hashes. A finding bead covers field-parsed act
  records whose prose has been altered". This report is that finding bead's
  output. Act records are cited by path; no act argument is quoted.

## Outcome

- [Observed] The gate recognizes the act records it reads in three ways. Of the
  15 records present in the decisions directory that its readers name, 6 are
  **field-parsed** (the reader reads the title and head field lines and nothing
  else), 5 are **byte-exact** (the record must be what its recorder's template
  renders from the slots read out of it) and 4 are **digest-pinned** (the whole
  file must hash to a constant in code).
- [Observed] Over the 6 field-parsed records, 337 of 385 non-blank lines can
  carry any appended text, and 327 can be deleted outright, without changing the
  reader's answer: the whole Ceremony, Effect and limits sections, and the head
  lines no reader parses. Over the 5 byte-exact records the figures are 3 of 265
  and 0, and over the 4 digest-pinned records 0 of 250 and 0.
- [Observed] With its Effect paragraph replaced by a grant of everything, its
  limits section removed, the owner's selection replaced by `Decline`, or a
  section withdrawing the act appended to the record itself, every field-parsed
  record still counts, in the characterization tests (recorder-rendered
  fixtures) and on all six real records. Every byte-exact record refuses each
  of those except the selection, which is a template slot; the registry sign-off
  also refuses the selection, because its reader requires the Scope A words. A
  `Date:` field alteration, the control, refuses in every family.
- [Observed] Each of the 6 field-parsed records is today exactly what its
  recorder renders: 3 are regenerated by a CI `--check` line (2 in the canonical
  battery and its hosted twin, 1 only in a hosted workflow), and the other 3,
  the superseded screening-scope versions 1–3, regenerate exactly in the probe
  once the policy bytes their acts bound are put back. No CI line regenerates
  versions 1–3.
- [Inferred] Recommendation: keep field parsing at runtime (the owner's GP-Q0
  status quo) and let bead `2.2`'s frozen act ledger, which hashes every act
  record, close the CI gap for versions 1–3. Tightening is costed below as an
  owner decision, not taken.

## The three recognition models

| Model | Readers | What must hold for a record to count |
|---|---|---|
| Field-parsed | `parseAct` (admission), `parsePolicyAct` (screening-scope chain), `readClassAct` (RFC5-14 class act), `readDigestBoundActState` for a form with no template (registry git-source; `recorderLines` adds the owner, provenance and A1 lines) | The title line; exactly one of each head field (`Date:`, `Act identity:`, `Act type:`, `Artifact identity:`, `Project identity:`, `Exact digest (SHA-256):`, the recording instant) in its exact form; the identity and type the form names; the digest equal to the bound artifact's bytes (an admission act whose digest differs is read as not in force) |
| Byte-exact | `readDigestBoundActState` with a template (`sittingTemplate`, `PROVIDER_V2_TEMPLATE` in `packages/polaris-dossier/src/gate-sources.ts`); `readVersionedSignoffState` (`registrySignoffTemplate`) | The slots read out of the record render the template back to the record byte for byte (`templateFields` in `packages/polaris-generation-consent/src/recorder-template.ts`), then the same head-field checks |
| Digest-pinned | `OWNER_ANSWERS_SHA256` and `HISTORICAL_POLICY_ACTS` in `packages/polaris-generation-consent/src/package-reader.ts` | The file's sha256 equals the constant |

[Observed] No gate decision reads a field-parsed record's prose. The readers
return type, identity, artifact, digest, date, instant and the supersession
field; the one runtime consumer that re-reads the record text,
`loadPublicSourceScreen` in
`apps/three-surface-poc/src/polaris-generation/public-source-screening.ts`,
checks the same head fields again (title, identity, date, act type, artifact,
project, exact digest) and nothing else.

## Per family

Denominators: forms are the act records each family's code names (a closed
list); present is how many of those exist at the measured commit. A record
counts as covered by CI when a `--check` line naming its recorder (and its act
key, for a multi-act recorder) appears in the canonical battery block of
`PROJECT-STATUS.md` or in `.github/workflows/governance-docs.yml` or
`.github/workflows/node-ci.yml`; every such line was run at the measured
commit and every one exited 0 (exit status and last output line per line in
`records[].checkLines`; the command text, which carries the act's argument, is
not copied).

[Observed] Versions 2 and 3 of the screening-scope chain each had a battery
line and lost it: version 2's was added with its record (`3409ef5a`) and

[Observed] `REGISTRY_GIT_SOURCE_ACT_FORM` and `readInForceEgress` (the egress
half of the admission family) have no non-test consumer outside the package

## What each reader sees (line census)

For every present record the census alters each non-blank line on its own,
once by appending ` [altered]` and once by deleting it, and reads the result
through the reader the gate uses, over an in-memory tree holding the record,
its bound files and its sibling records, at a fixed instant. A line is unseen
when the reader's answer does not change.

| Model | Records | Non-blank lines | Unseen on append | Unseen on delete |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Field-parsed | 6 | 385 | 337 | 327 |
| Byte-exact | 5 | 265 | 3 | 0 |
| Digest-pinned | 4 | 250 | 0 | 0 |

[Observed] Per field-parsed record, every Ceremony, Effect and limits line is
unseen both ways; the seen lines are the title and the head fields the readers
parse. The other head lines (owner, provenance state, supersession text, A1)
are unseen as well, except where a reader holds part of one: the policy chain
matches the provenance-state line's prefix in all four versions, and in versions
2–4 needs the supersession field to name the earlier version, so appending to
those lines (4 versions × 1 line, plus 3 × 2 supersession lines) is unseen but
deleting them is not. That accounts for the 10 lines between 337 and 327.
Per-record and per-section counts and line numbers are in `records[].sections`,
`records[].unseenLinesOnAppend` and `records[].unseenLinesOnDelete` of the
companion record.

[Observed] The byte-exact residual: the three unseen-on-append lines are all in
the registry sign-off's head (`Owner selection:`, `Review:` and `Disposition:`,
each a line slot that ends its line). The sitting and provider templates end
every slot line with literal text, so an appended suffix is seen there; a slot's
own content is still free text within its pattern (the selection alteration
below).

## Rule 6: the characterization tests fail when the gate changes

[Observed] Two records written by `scripts/rule6_mutation_runner.py`, one per
test project (`docs/evidence/consent-gate-recognition-models-mutants-consent-2026-10-21.json`,
25 mutants; `docs/evidence/consent-gate-recognition-models-mutants-dossier-2026-10-21.json`,
18), each carrying its subject commit and every mutant's `old`/`new` fragment,
hold 43 mutants, all killed:

- 16 make a field-parsed reader read one part of its prose (each of the four
  readers refuses a record whose Effect does not open with its recorder's
  words, whose limits heading is missing, whose selection label is `Decline` or
  `Reject`, or which carries a `## Withdrawal` section), and 4 drop the end
  anchor from each reader's `Date:` pattern; each is killed by exactly 1 test.
- 13 loosen or narrow a byte-exact template or the sign-off reader: the Effect
  (or What-this-records) paragraph, the limits section and the record's end each
  made a free slot in the sitting, provider v2 and sign-off templates; the label
  slot made to refuse `Decline` in the sitting and provider v2 templates; the
  sign-off's "Extend Scope A" requirement dropped; the sign-off's selection also
  required to name its version.
- 10 make one alteration (or the control) a no-op in the test support, once per
  test project, so that each test's "applies" guard or control verdict must
  fail.

A mutant counts as killed when at least one test in the named files fails, so
a mutant an existing test also catches (the template mutants fail up to 5)
would count even if the new tests missed it. [Observed] Re-running each of the
43 from its record (same `old`/`new`, file and project) with only the 14 new
tests selected (`-t syzygy-bpyt.8.10`) fails at least one new test every time:
each of the 20 field-parsed and 13 template mutants fails exactly one, the test
for the family it mutates, and each no-op alteration fails each new test whose
record it then leaves unchanged (1 to 4 tests; the sign-off's `What this
records` heading and `Owner selection:` line are reached by patterns the
effect and selection mutants leave intact).

## Residuals

1. [Observed] Byte-exact recognition fixes every literal line, not the slots.
   The owner-selection slots (`opening`, `label`, `description`, and `quote`
   where a template has one) accept any one line that carries no 64-hex token;
   other owner words in them still count. The sign-off adds one content check
   (its quote names "Extend Scope A").
2. [Observed] The line slot's pattern is ambiguous, and a byte-exact read of a
   record that does not match backtracks heavily. `SLOT.line` is
   `[^\r\n]*\S[^\r\n]*`; a line of n non-space characters matches it n ways,
   and the ways multiply across the slots before a failing literal. The real
   provider v2 record's four selection slots hold 292, 28, 154 and 54
   characters. In the census, its 128 reads had a median of 41 s and a slowest
   of 232 s (`records[].readTiming`: wall time in one of 11 worker threads, all
   busy), and its census took 890 s against under 8 s for every other record.
   Measured alone (`docs/evidence/consent-gate-slot-line-timing-2026-10-21.json`,
   from `scripts/measure_slot_line_backtracking.mjs`), one read of that record
   with an alteration after the slots took 30 to 93 s with `SLOT.line` and 0 ms
   with `[^\S\r\n]*\S[^\r\n]*` (blanks other than CR and LF, then the first
   non-space character, then anything but CR and LF: the same lines, one way
   each), with the same match verdict in all 7 cases. An alteration before the
   first slot (the `Date:` control) fails in 0 ms either way. This is
   availability, not acceptance: every such read still refuses. Changing the
   pattern changes the consent gate's recognition code (class 4) and is a
   follow-up, not part of this bead.
3. [Inferred] A CI hash or regeneration check covers only what is committed
   and pushed. The readers take a filesystem root and read the records there,
   so a prose edit to a field-parsed record in an operator's checkout counts at
   runtime whether or not CI ever sees it, and an in-place withdrawal section is
   ignored by the reader while a person reading the record would take the act
   as withdrawn. Lawful withdrawal is a separate later act, which the readers'
   sweeps do catch [Observed: for example "withdrawal and forgery forms the reader does not define" in `packages/polaris-generation-consent/src/package-reader.test.ts`].
4. [Inferred] No model authenticates a record against forgery in the working
   tree: a record written from scratch with the right head fields (field-parsed)
   or rendered from the template with chosen slots (byte-exact) counts, and the
   digest pins sit in code in the same tree. Recognition guards against drift
   and mistakes, not against someone who can edit the checkout.
5. [Unknown] The PWB owner-act parser
   (`packages/three-surface-poc-core/src/owner-act-record.ts`, evaluated by
   `packages/three-surface-poc-core/src/body-read-authority.ts`) parses its own
   fields, including the Ceremony phrase and the Effect section, and was not
   measured here.
6. [Observed] Bead `syzygy-bpyt.8.4`'s description places the parser families
   in packages/polaris-dossier/src/package-reader.ts; at the measured commit
   they are in `packages/polaris-generation-consent/src/package-reader.ts`, and
   the dossier path does not exist.

## Proposal: keep, or tighten to byte-exact

A "template copy" is the second copy of a recorder's render that byte-exact
recognition needs: a TypeScript template beside the Python `render_act`, held
to it by an oracle test like "the gate forms against the recorders" in
`packages/polaris-dossier/src/gate-acts.test.ts`. The three existing templates
are 84, 90 and 73 lines.

| Option | What changes | Cost | Fit with 8.4 and 2.2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **K — keep** (GP-Q0 status quo) | Nothing at runtime. Bead `2.2`'s ledger records each act record's path and sha256 and replaces the regenerating battery lines with its hash check, so CI hashes all six field-parsed records, including versions 1–3 that no line regenerates today | 0 template copies; depends on `2.2` landing | Unchanged: 8.4 converges the field-parsed families as rows with no template; 2.2 is the CI coverage |
| **T1 — tighten to byte-exact templates** | Each field-parsed form gets a template; prose alterations refuse at runtime, slots excepted | 8 template copies, one per recorder `render_act` (admission acts, egress v2, screening-scope v1, v2, v3, v4, RFC5-14 class act, registry entries), each with an oracle case and rule-6 mutants; about 8 × 80 lines [Inferred]. 7 if the unused registry form is retired instead. The v3 and v4 records carry variants and packet words, so their templates need parameters. Prerequisite: residual 2, or each new template adds slow failing reads | Compatible: 8.4's rows already allow an optional template, and T1 moves forms toward byte-exact, never away; 2.2 unaffected |
| **T2 — pin each record's digest in code** | Like `HISTORICAL_POLICY_ACTS`, one sha256 constant per present record | 6 constants now, and one in the recording commit of every future act of these families: recording an act would then require a code change, which changes the act ceremony | Compatible with 8.4 (a pin is a row attribute); duplicates 2.2's ledger hashes in code. A variant that reads 2.2's ledger at runtime would make a file declared "a record, never authority" load-bearing |

[Inferred] Recommendation: K. Field parsing reads exactly what the gate
decides on, all six records are recorder-exact today, and 2.2 gives every one a
CI hash at no template cost; the remaining exposure is residual 3, a working-tree
edit between CI runs. Choose T1 if the owner wants a record's human-readable
prose (its Effect, limits and any withdrawal text) to be held at runtime too,
fixing residual 2 first. T1 and T2 each change what counts as an act and return
to the owner as a decision under `syzygy-bpyt.1`; this report takes neither.

## How to re-run

```sh
npx tsc -b packages/polaris-generation-consent packages/polaris-dossier
node scripts/measure_consent_gate_recognition.mjs --out <file.json>
npx vitest run --project @syzygy/polaris-generation-consent packages/polaris-generation-consent/src/package-reader.test.ts packages/polaris-generation-consent/src/digest-bound-act.test.ts -t syzygy-bpyt.8.10
npx vitest run --project @syzygy/polaris-dossier packages/polaris-dossier/src/gate-acts.test.ts -t syzygy-bpyt.8.10
```

The census took about 15 minutes on this host, 890 s of it the provider v2
record (residual 2). Every field but `readTiming` and `pool` reproduces at the
same commit; the timing probe's verdicts reproduce, its timings do not.
